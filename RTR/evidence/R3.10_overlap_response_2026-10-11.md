## Reviewer comment (verbatim)

> The overlap-removal strategy may discard biologically relevant alternatives. The greedy procedure
> retains the candidate with the longest arm within overlapping groups, using shorter spacer length as
> a tie-breaker. It is not clear that longest-arm length is always the biologically optimal criterion,
> particularly when a shorter overlapping motif has a substantially higher predicted stability. The
> authors should justify this choice and compare it with alternatives such as selecting the
> highest-scoring candidate. Since the web interface apparently permits overlapping motifs to be
> retained, the manuscript should also explain which mode is recommended for biological analysis.

## Response text

We thank the reviewer for this comment. We agree that arm length is not a validated proxy for
biological activity, and we now offer the alternative the reviewer proposes and compare it with the
longest-arm rule. We keep the longest-arm rule as the default for three reasons. First, among
overlapping candidates that share a mirror-repeat region, the longest arm describes the full extent
of the repeat, which is the region that can take part in strand exchange. Second, it is a purely
geometric criterion: it does not depend on the stability score, which, as we explain in response to
comment 4, is a heuristic rather than a calibrated free energy, so small score differences between
overlapping candidates should not decide what a user sees by default. Third, it is faster on genomes,
because overlaps are resolved before candidates are scored. HSeeker remains a deterministic detector
of mirror repeats within homopurine/homopyrimidine tracts; it does not predict which conformation a
given locus adopts in vivo.

We now provide three overlap modes in the Python API, CLI, and web interface:

- **Greedy selection (default):** a start-ordered sweep in which a candidate with a longer arm
  replaces the current overlapping candidate; a shorter spacer breaks ties. Overlaps are resolved
  before scoring.
- **Score-based selection:** `overlap_strategy="score"` (CLI: `-overlap-strategy score`; web:
  *Highest scores first*) scores every candidate first. Candidates are considered in descending
  `total_score` order and kept when they do not overlap a previously kept candidate on the same
  sequence. Ties favour longer arms, then shorter spacers, then earlier coordinates. The result is a
  deterministic maximal set of non-overlapping intervals; the rule does not claim to maximise the
  number of hits or their combined score.
- **Retain all candidates:** `remove_overlaps=False` (CLI: `-skipoverlap`; web: *Keep all raw
  hits*) returns every candidate that passes the filters.

Coordinates are 1-based and inclusive.

**Filter order.** The AT-content filter runs before scoring. With homopolymer filtering on, the
unscored path checks `full_sequence`; the scored path checks `full_sequence` and
`putative_triplex`. Greedy selection runs before scoring and the scored homopolymer check;
score-based selection runs after them, so if a candidate is filtered out, a lower-scoring
overlapping alternative can still be kept.

**Recommended use.**
- *Genome-wide annotation:* the default (greedy). It gives one non-redundant motif per region and is
  the fastest mode.
- *Locus-level work,* such as choosing which variant to test experimentally: score-based selection,
  which reports the overlapping variant with the highest heuristic score.
- *Exhaustive or downstream statistical analysis:* retain all candidates and review the alternatives.

A higher heuristic score helps prioritise candidates but is not experimental evidence of H-DNA
formation.

**Illustrative examples.** Each input below gives exactly two overlapping candidates with scoring on
and `minrep=7, maxrep=25, maxspacer=5, purity=0.9, mismatch=0.2, at_threshold=0.8,
filter_homopolymers=True`.

| Input | Candidate | Interval | Arm / spacer | Total score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| agreement `GAGAAAAGAGGGAGAAAAAGGAA` | gagaaaagagggagaaaaagg | 1–21 | 10 / 1 | 53.635 |
| agreement | gaaaagagggagaaaaa | 3–19 | 8 / 1 | 53.635 |
| difference `GGAAGAAGAAGGAGGAGAGAGGGAAGGAA` | ggaagaagaaggagg | 1–15 | 7 / 1 | 38.575 |
| difference | aaggaggagagagggaaggaa | 9–29 | 10 / 1 | 20.360 |

For *agreement*, both rules keep 1–21: greedy because its arm is longer, score-based selection
because the two scores tie and the longer arm wins the tie. For *difference*, greedy keeps 9–29 (arm
10 rather than 7), while score-based selection keeps 1–15 (38.575 versus 20.360). Retaining overlaps
returns both candidates.

**Overlap chains.** An overlap chain need not collapse to one representative. Scanning `"GAA" * 20`
with `minrep=10, maxrep=15, maxspacer=10, purity=0.9, mismatch=0.1, at_threshold=0.8` and homopolymer
filtering yields 102 overlapping candidates; both rules keep two compatible hits, 3–32 (score 146.30)
and 35–60 (score 124.87). A regression test checks a five-candidate chain in which score ordering
yields either 1–3–5 or 2–4, and a randomised reference test checks the priority rule.

**Comparison on the benchmark.** With the shipped defaults (`minrep=8, maxrep=1000, maxspacer=10,
purity=0.90, mismatch=0.10, at_threshold=0.80`, homopolymer filtering on), 43 of the 70 experimental
records have overlapping candidates after filtering. Greedy and score-based selection return
identical intervals for 38 of them and different intervals for 5 (HDNA0040, HDNA0044, HDNA0049,
HDNA0050, HDNA0051). The detection call (at least one hit) is the same for all 130 benchmark
records, and for all 74,880 record-by-configuration calls across the 576 parameter and filter
combinations of our robustness grid. None of the reported performance figures therefore depends on
the overlap rule.

**Comparison on human chromosome 1 (hg38).** With the CLI defaults, greedy selection reports 45,927
loci and score-based selection 47,217. Every greedy locus is overlapped by a score-selected locus,
and 41,867 (91.2 %) have identical coordinates. Score-based selection reports 991 additional loci
that do not overlap any greedy locus. Without the composition filters, the counts are 106,481 and
108,224 (82.6 % identical, 100 % of greedy loci covered, 1,213 score-only loci). The two rules
therefore find the same regions and differ mainly in which representation of a region they report.
