## R2.3 (verbatim)

> those cases. Also, were there any other cases obtained in the modified E.Coli genome? In general, to
> strengthen the analysis, the authors might run their tool not on the modified E.Coli genome, but on a
> genome which was tested to contain non B-DNA sequences. For example in the paper of Kouzine (ref 4)
> such a analysis was done. If the set of sequences found by HSeek would be a subset of all non-B-DNA
> sequences, that would be a biologically sound result!

## Response to R2.3

We agree that the E. coli insertion experiment tested recovery of known sequences in a genomic
background, not H-DNA formation, and we have removed it from the manuscript (see also our response to
Reviewer 3, comment 7). Following the reviewer's suggestion, we instead compared HSeeker with the
genome-wide data of Kouzine et al. (2017). They mapped single-stranded DNA in vivo with permanganate
and S1 nuclease footprinting in activated mouse B cells and human Raji cells, predicted non-B DNA
motifs genome-wide, and called a motif formed ("ssDNA+") when it carried single-stranded signal. We
used their published motif calls and signal tracks, on the genome builds they used (mm9, hg19), with
no reprocessing of our own, and scanned both genomes with HSeeker at its default settings.

**Recall.** HSeeker loci overlap 79.0 % of the H-DNA structures the authors found formed in mouse
(13,517 of 17,109) and 82.3 % in human Raji cells (6,879 of 8,356).

**Selectivity.** Among the authors' own predicted H-DNA motifs, those that HSeeker calls are formed
more often than those it does not: 4.5 % versus 2.8 % in mouse (odds ratio 1.65, 95 % CI 1.59–1.71)
and 5.7 % versus 1.7 % in human (3.39, 3.20–3.59). Restricting to motifs that the 36-nt reads can map
to, the odds ratios are 1.68–2.07 in mouse and 4.16–5.70 in human.

**On the subset criterion.** A strict subset is not attainable by any sequence-based predictor on
these data, because formation depends on cell state: only about 3–5 % of the authors' own predicted
H-DNA motifs were found single-stranded in a given cell type. The informative questions are
therefore whether HSeeker's calls are enriched for structures that form, which they are, and how many
of the formed structures HSeeker finds, which is about four in five.

**What HSeeker misses.** Most of the formed H-DNA that HSeeker does not report are short A/T-rich or
single-base motifs, which the default composition filters exclude by design. In the authors' data,
this class is found single-stranded at about half the rate of other predicted H-DNA in mouse and a
quarter in human, which supports excluding it by default; it is nevertheless 17–19 % of the formed
H-DNA, and users can disable the filters (`-at-threshold`), at a cost in selectivity.

Finally, as Reviewer 3 asked in comment 8, we tested whether the measured single-stranded signal is
higher at HSeeker loci than at composition-matched DNA without a mirror repeat; see our response there.

## R3.8 (verbatim)

> A genome-scale biological validation would substantially strengthen the manuscript. HSeeker
> identifies 96,729 non-overlapping H-DNA loci on chromosome 1 under default parameters. Yet there is
> little analysis demonstrating that these predictions correspond to independent experimental
> signatures of non-B DNA formation or biologically meaningful genomic features. This is a missed
> opportunity. For a bioinformatics paper claiming genome-scale annotation utility, the authors should
> ideally compare HSeeker predictions with an independent genome-wide experimental dataset, for example
> structure-sensitive nuclease mapping or another suitable non-B-DNA dataset, while carefully
> accounting for sequence composition. Even a well-designed enrichment analysis against independent
> experimental loci would provide an important orthogonal validation.

## Response to R3.8

We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. We compared HSeeker predictions with the structure-sensitive
nuclease mapping of Kouzine et al. (2017): permanganate/S1 footprinting of single-stranded DNA in
activated and resting mouse B cells and in human Raji cells. The recall and selectivity results against
the authors' own calls are described in our response to Reviewer 2, comment 3. Here we describe two
enrichment analyses of the measured single-stranded signal. The first was specified, with its controls
and statistics, before any result was computed. The second was added after the first human result and
was likewise specified before it was computed. We report both, and we deposit the design document with
every change and its reason.

**Composition matching.** Single-stranded signal depends strongly on base composition. Controls were
therefore matched to each HSeeker locus on length, A/T (equivalently G/C) count and purine/pyrimidine
asymmetry, and did not overlap any HSeeker locus or predicted H-DNA motif. Matching on asymmetry is
essential: controls matched on A/T content alone carry 1.4–3 times more signal than HSeeker loci,
because mixed-sequence DNA gives more single-stranded signal than purine/pyrimidine-asymmetric DNA of
the same composition.

**First analysis: all HSeeker loci, genome-wide controls.** In activated mouse B cells, HSeeker loci
carried 14 % more single-stranded signal than matched controls (ratio of means 1.14, 95 % CI
1.13–1.15; 825,749 loci). In resting B cells the ratio was 0.82 (0.82–0.83), and in human Raji cells
0.98 (0.97–0.98). This analysis does not separate H-DNA from other sources of single-stranded DNA
(transcription bubbles, G-quadruplexes, Z-DNA, cruciforms, duplex melting) and does not account for
the poor mappability of repetitive purine tracts with 36-nt reads.

**Second analysis: loci isolated from other sources of single-stranded DNA.** We removed, from loci and
controls alike, every interval the reads cannot map to, every interval within 100 bp of a motif the
authors predicted as G-quadruplex, Z-DNA, cruciform or stress-induced melting site, and every interval
within 2 kb of an annotated transcription start site, and drew ten controls per locus within 50 kb (the
same chromatin and transcriptional context). In activated mouse B cells, isolated HSeeker loci carried
25 % more single-stranded signal than their controls (1.25, 1.18–1.33; a locus exceeded one of its own
controls with probability 0.514, p = 2 × 10⁻¹⁷). In human Raji cells there was no detectable excess
(1.02, 0.99–1.06; probability 0.503, p = 0.08). Two built-in checks behaved as required in both species:
a placebo comparison of controls against controls showed no difference (probability 0.500–0.502), and
the motifs the authors called formed were enriched under the same procedure (7.4-fold in mouse, 2.1-fold
in human).

**Dependence on the mirror repeat and on cell state.** G-quadruplexes, Z-DNA, melting and transcription
do not depend on mirror symmetry once composition is matched, whereas H-DNA stability increases with arm
length. In activated mouse B cells the excess increased with arm length, from none detectable at 8–9 nt
(1.12, 0.93–1.35) to 1.58 (1.34–1.83) at 12–14 nt and 1.6–1.8 at longer arms (Spearman p = 3 × 10⁻²¹).
The enrichment was absent in resting B cells (0.86, 0.83–0.89), in which even the authors' formed motifs
showed no excess, consistent with the activation- and supercoiling-dependent formation reported by
Kouzine et al. In Raji cells no arm-length class differed from 1.

**Interpretation and limitations.** In activated mouse B cells, HSeeker loci are associated with
single-stranded DNA beyond what composition, mappability, local context, the other predicted non-B
structures and promoter transcription explain, and the association grows with mirror-repeat arm length
as H-DNA formation should. We did not observe this enrichment in human Raji cells. There, HSeeker's
calls are still strongly enriched among the motifs the authors found formed (odds ratio 3.39; see our
response to Reviewer 2), but the signal at HSeeker loci as a whole is not higher than at matched
non-mirror DNA. The Raji data have no input control and a smaller dynamic range (the authors' formed
motifs are enriched 2.1-fold against 7.4-fold in mouse). The per-locus effect is small even in mouse:
most HSeeker loci, like most controls, carry no single-stranded signal in a given cell state, so HSeeker
loci are candidates rather than sites shown to be structured. Single-stranded signal does not prove
triplex formation at an individual locus, and unannotated transcription and structures missed by the
authors' predictors are not controlled. We added both analyses to the Results, with a figure that
replaces the former Figure 3, and these limitations to the Discussion. All code and intermediate tables
are deposited (`analysis/scripts/run_kouzine_published.py`, `kouzine_hdna_isolated.py`,
`analysis/results/kouzine_v1/`).

We also note that the chromosome 1 count in the comment (96,729) referred to the previous default
minimum arm length of 10 nt. With the current defaults (minimum arm 8 nt), HSeeker reports 45,927 loci
on hg38 chromosome 1 with the command-line defaults and 106,481 without the composition filters, and
the manuscript reports both.
