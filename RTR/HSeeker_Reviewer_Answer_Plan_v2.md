- **DEC-9 — overlap removal defaulted to Nikol's score-informed strategy; default superseded 2026-10-08 by DEC-10** (evidence: `RTR/evidence/DEC-9_score_overlap_default_and_optimisation.md`). From 2026-10-07 until DEC-10, the default for `scan_sequence`, `scan_fasta`, `scan_fasta_iter` and `scan_fasta_parallel` was `overlap_strategy=None`. It resolved to `"score"` (keep the highest-scoring hit in each overlapping cluster) when `score=True`, and to `"greedy"` (the earlier longest-arm rule) when `score=False`, since there is nothing to rank by. The CLI behaved the same way (`-overlap-strategy` defaulted to score; `-no-score` gave greedy), and the webapp form defaulted to score and fell back to greedy when scoring was unchecked. **DEC-10 reverted these defaults to greedy; what remains of DEC-9 is score mode as an option, its optimisation and the effect analysis below.** An explicit `"score"` with `score=False` still raises `ValueError`. `hseeker.ablation` keeps its explicit per-model handling. **Effect:** no detection call changed on the benchmark (0 binary-call differences greedy vs score across the full WS4-A3 grid: 576 configurations × 128 records = 73,728 calls, all four filter states; 0/128 at the reported configuration), so no reported metric moves. In general a call *can* differ when the homopolymer filter is on: greedy's longest-arm pick may be a pure homopolymer that the filter then drops, leaving no hit, whereas score selection can report an overlapping near-homopolymer candidate (e.g. ACGT×10 + G×100 + C×100 + ACGT×10 with the CLI filters: 0 hits under greedy, 3 under score). Without that filter a call cannot change, because score selection keeps the top-scoring candidate. **Which hit is reported in an overlapping cluster can change** (e.g. GAMIR_SEQ: score keeps the higher-scoring arm-6 hit, greedy the arm-7 hit). Score-based selection was also **optimised with the logic kept the same**: incremental window search (per-hit cost O(arm³) → O(arm²), with float results guarded by tests), an O(arm) score upper bound, and lazy score-ordered selection. On poly-G 400 bp it now takes about 0.06 s, where it took 96 s before. It is still slower than greedy on long homopolymer-rich input (e.g. 5.4 s vs 1.6 s on G3000+C2000+A5000) and slightly slower on a 2 Mb test genome (8.8 s vs 7.8 s), but on a real genome it costs about 2.3× greedy (hg38 chr1, 16 workers: ~60 s vs 26.3 s; `RTR/evidence/WS6-A4_chr1_runtime_memory_2026-10-07.md`), which is why DEC-10 restored greedy as the default. Test suite: 344 passed, 3 skipped (2026-10-08). The optimisation was **verified to give identical results to the pre-optimisation code** (one-off side-by-side harness, 2026-10-07: 70,984 candidates × 4 flag combinations in the scorer, 19,710 `scan_sequence` calls, all FASTA and parallel paths, and long runs — all identical; DEC-9 evidence §4). Remaining worst case (score mode only): long G/C homopolymer runs with the homopolymer filter on (G400+C400: 40 s under score vs 0.02 s under greedy; cost grows roughly with the cube of the run length); greedy, the default again since DEC-10, avoids it. ### A.3 Benchmark-validity issues found by the audit (absent from plan v1)

1. **HDNA0026 (R2-ino) — encoding artifact.** R2-ino is an *inosine-substituted* oligo (Del Mundo 2017, confirmed in paper text: third-strand guanines → inosine; TM ≈ 80 °C hairpin control). The benchmark FASTA silently converts inosine→G, manufacturing the all-G R2 sequence — a perfect mirror repeat. This produced **1 of the 2 false positives** in the current repo run. **Disposition: excluded** from the ACGT sequence-level benchmark (the label belongs to a chemically modified oligo, not to any DNA sequence).
2. **HDNA0080 (G10TTAA_AG5) — label-scope error.** The record's own notes: "NOT intramolecular H-DNA; G10 forms intermolecular triplex with AG5 TFO; correctly not detected by HSeeker." It is labeled `forming`, so it counts as a false negative by construction. **Disposition: excluded** (outside the tool's intramolecular target class; optionally cited in text as a target-class boundary example).
3. **HDNA0054 (poly-(dC)30) — by-design exclusion.** Forming per Kohwi 1988 (PMID 3375241, verified), but a pure homopolymer that the production CLI removes (`filter_homopolymers=True`, now `__main__.py:120`). ~~Keep in primary **only** for configurations that disable the homopolymer filter~~ — **superseded 2026-10-02 (DEC-7): removed from the benchmark** together with the other five pure homopolymers (`curation_decision=removed`). The tension itself (the production filter rejects a literature-forming sequence) still has to be disclosed in Methods/Limitations (see register D-8).
4. **HDNA0029 (pCON) — kept as published (author decision DEC-13, 2026-10-09).** The audit read Wang 2004 Fig. 1A as showing a terminal C that the record lacks (`TTGCTGAACTTGATTCGACTCAGATGCAGTGC`) and proposed adding it. (Its lengths were each one short: the record is 31 nt, and with the terminal C it would be 32 nt.) The authors keep the sequence exactly as in the submitted manuscript's Supplementary Table: 31 nt `TTGCTGAACTTGATTCGACTCAGATGCAGTG`, identical in the original `hdna_experimental_sequences_final.csv` and in v3. HSeeker calls pCON non-forming with or without the terminal C (0 hits under the script, manuscript and shipped configurations), so no result depends on it. The correction is **not** applied, and no text may say that the pCON sequence was fixed or corrected.
5. **HDNA0055 (GA32) — citation correction.** Sequence matches Rooney & Moore 1995 GA32 (30-nt) exactly; the CSV cites Mirkin 1987 (motif origin) and Wang 2004's GA32 is a different 31-nt sequence. Add Rooney 1995 as sequence-source citation.
